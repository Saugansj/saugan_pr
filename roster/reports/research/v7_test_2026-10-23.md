# btc-speed v7 test (written 22-23 Oct 2026 IST, paper only, nothing started)

v7 = v4's 2 s / 6 bp Bybit rule with v6's two skips replaced by one fair-value (FV) rule: buy the favoured side only
when its ask is at most FV - taker fee - margin m, and cap the limit there too. FV = Phi(ln(S/K) / (c sigma sqrt(tau))):
S the ref price now, K the 60 s ref TWAP before the window start, sigma from 30-min realised volatility, tau the time
left. Three values were fitted, on early data only: c (a scale on the volatility), m (the margin) and k (for the
volatility-scaled trigger).

Replay P&L is an upper bound: no race with other bots, 5 shares always at the ask.

**What this means.** FV is real information: it predicts the outcome better than the market's own ask in every data set
(log-loss FV vs ask: archive in sample 0.420 vs 0.452, out of sample 0.454 vs 0.479 on 1,261 decisions, live test half
0.616 vs 0.645 on 210 trades; within the same ask band, trades with FV above the ask win more often). As a filter it
raises the profit per trade (replay +0.74 vs +0.54) and takes a third to a half fewer trades, so the total stays about
where v4's is: it beats v4 on neither the replay nor the live estimate. On BTC it is ahead of v6 on the replay (+77 ± 42)
and on the live estimate overall (+8 ± 16; behind it in the live fit half, -7 ± 7), all under 2 SE. On ETH it is worse.

**Recommendation.** Start **paper_v7b (BTC) as a paper run**, beside paper_v6b: on BTC it passed the pre-registered
replay test and is ahead of v6 there (+77 ± 42) and on the live estimate overall (+8 ± 16), with fewer trades, and its rows log fv, edge, K and sigma so
the run can be audited. Do **not** start paper_eth7b: ETH's live estimate under v7 is about zero against v4's +23.7,
and there is no ETH archive to check it on. The **0.70 cap** had the best live estimate of any rule, in both halves and
both coins (+71.2 vs +45.2 for v4), but it loses on the replay (in sample +49.8 vs +82.7; out of sample -19.9 ± 22.5),
which has no race; it was not built as a run (that needs one more flag); if you want an ETH change, it is the better
candidate than eth7b. The volatility-scaled trigger was **not built** (below). Nothing was started and nothing was added
to keeper ACTIVE: `ops\paper_tasks.ps1 startv7b` starts the BTC run once you approve it.

## The volatility-scaled trigger (plan item 3): not built

Fire when the 2 s move >= max(4 bp, k sigma_2s). In sample it lost to the fixed 6 bp at every k (at the matched k 8:
v4 rule +32.6 vs +82.7, v7 rule +60.1 vs +90.8). Out of sample it won (v4 rule +426.6 vs +347.4; v7+vol - v7 +37.2 ±
43.1, under 1 SE). Opposite signs on the two archive sets, and it cannot be checked on the live trades (it changes
which signals exist), so the evidence does not support adding it; the engine keeps the 6 bp trigger. The detector
gained an optional per-tick threshold (MoveDetector.update(..., bp=None)) for this test; existing callers are unchanged.

## Sizing (plan item 5)

5 shares flat, no compounding, unchanged. Each paper run trades one coin, so the "BTC + ETH at the same moment = one
bet" rule never binds inside a run on paper; it matters only if BTC and ETH are later traded from one live account.
Nothing built.

## What was not tested

- The vol trigger on live trades (impossible offline) and any ETH archive replay (no ETH archive books on disk).
- K is the Bybit spot TWAP, a proxy for Polymarket's Chainlink start price; it was not compared with the published
  priceToBeat. The model also ignores that settlement is itself a 60 s TWAP at the close (c absorbs part of it).
- The live estimate's race share (RACE_AHEAD 0.31) was fitted on the archive at 500 ms; a lower limit changes fills only
  through that model. Trades without real tape prints in their second drop out of every rule alike.
