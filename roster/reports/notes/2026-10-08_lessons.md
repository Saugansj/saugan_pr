## Lessons

1. **Hold vs quick exit depends on the regime.**
   - Days 1–2 and today's daytime reverted: every v2 trade was in profit seconds after the fill and lost at settlement (a 5 s exit would have made +$1.33 today and +$7.21 over two days).
   - The evening (20:45–22:30) trended: v3's 11 trades made −$0.39 with the quick exit, and **+$7.53 held**.
   - Neither fixed rule is right. The next version must know which regime it is in, or the choice must be shown to be right on average over many days. Three paper days cannot settle this; replaying history can.
2. **Most of the missed money was in signals we never traded.**
   - The pauses blocked 27 v2 signals, which held would have made **+$18.81**.
   - The stale-signal guard skipped 50 market-signals across both runs; **43 would have won**.
   - Many skips came from our own event-loop backlog, not slow data (fixed at 22:56). Even so, in a trend the edge lasts longer than the 1 s guard assumes.
3. **A slow consumer looks like a stale signal.**
   - The process handled Polymarket messages (about 1,700/s) ahead of Binance ticks, so ticks queued for up to 20 s, and Polymarket dropped us 502 times.
   - Always measure the feed alone before blaming the network: Binance by itself had a p99 lag under 50 ms.
4. **Market making near the close is adverse selection.**
   - The one ladder fill came 41 minutes before close and 2 minutes after a sharp BTC move. It lost $5.84.
   - Ladder v2 now pulls quotes in the last hour and stays quiet for 5 minutes after a sharp move.
5. **The ladder's model shows no tradable edge yet.**
   - Only 2 resolved checkpoints had |fair − mid| > 3¢ (+$0.24 per share), and arbitrage was worth +$0.42.
   - With bids about 125 shares deep in the queue, the ladder rarely fills. Queue position needs studying before the ladder can earn.
6. **Paper evidence is too slow on its own.**
   - Each pre-registered test needs 200 trades. v2 has 3 after two days; v3 gets about 10 on a busy evening.
   - We record almost nothing of the 1,700 messages per second we receive, so skipped signals often have no price and can't be valued.
   - The research layer needs history and a replay, not more days of waiting.
7. **The PC sleeping still pauses the runs.** This is left as is by choice; the 08:15 task now clears breaker pauses (never a halt).

## What changes because of this

The research-layer fix plan: `build/research-layer/plan.md`. It covers recorded and historical data, a signal replay over months of history, regime features, and a queue study for the ladder.

## Addendum, 9 Oct: the first replay (27 Jul to 9 Aug, `reports/research/replay_2026-07-27_2026-08-09.md`)

- 10 usable days, 136 signals. At the modelled latency (signal + 150 ms + 200 ms RTT), btc-speed made money in history: +$0.50 per trade with exits at 5–60 s (SE about $0.10), +$0.65 held (SE $0.24), cap 0.75.
- **The edge is gone within about 1 s.** Acting 1 s late: exits +$0.04 to +$0.18 per trade. 2 s late: negative. 5 s late: clearly negative. Speed is the strategy.
- The archive's receive clock runs about 135 ms (p50) to 320 ms (p99) behind exchange time. So the replay's "lag 0" is a little better than live: live arrival is about signal + 450–500 ms.
- **By regime:** the edge sits in calm markets (low 30-min volatility: about +$1.1 to +$1.4 per trade; high: about 0) and in isolated signals (no other signal in the last 30 min: +$1.0 to +$1.3; clustered signals: about 0). In this sample holding beat exiting when BTC was trending the same way over 15–60 min, and exiting was better in the middle tercile. Small n per bucket.
- Paper days 7–8 Oct lost money while the replay is positive. The likely reasons: the slow-consumer backlog (fixed 8 Oct), buys at 0.76–0.89, and clustered signals in volatile stretches. **The data says we under-recorded and over-reacted, not that the idea is dead.**
- Downloads must not run during trading hours: on 9 Oct the history download pushed Binance lag up to 6.3 s.
